# Purpose

This document records the chronological development of the Agro-Smart Hub project from concept generation to competition demonstration. It documents the major engineering activities, design changes, technical challenges, and lessons learned throughout the project.

---

# Development Timeline

## Phase 1 – Problem Identification

Growing up, I observed my grandparents processing cassava into gari in a rural farming community. Among the various stages of processing, the sieving operation stood out because it required repetitive manual effort to separate fine cassava particles from coarse particles before frying.

When the theme for the Battle of the Bots Robotics Competition 2.0, **"Robotics for Industrialization,"** was announced, I revisited this experience and identified cassava processing as an opportunity for agricultural automation. Rather than attempting to automate the entire production process, I selected the sieving stage as the primary engineering problem to solve.

**Outcome**

- Engineering problem identified.
- Initial project concept established.

---

## Phase 2 – System Concept Development

The initial concept expanded beyond an automated siever into an integrated mechatronic system.

To improve sustainability, a renewable energy system consisting of a solar panel and rechargeable battery pack was included. Material handling systems were added to reduce manual transportation, leading to the introduction of a electro-mechanical seiver, slant conveyor, flat conveyor, and mobile robotic truck.

The project gradually evolved into a complete agricultural automation prototype composed of multiple interacting subsystems.

**Outcome**

- Overall system architecture defined.
- Major subsystems identified.

---

## Phase 3 – Proposal Development

A detailed proposal was prepared according to the competition requirements.

The proposal included:

- project motivation,
- problem definition,
- proposed solution,
- SDG alignment,
- conceptual designs,
- component list,
- implementation timeline,
- expected impact.

The proposal was accepted, allowing the team to proceed to the construction phase.

**Outcome**

- Proposal approved.
- Project officially commenced.

---

## Phase 4 – Student Training

Before construction began, students received introductory training in engineering concepts required for the project.

Topics included:

- motion,
- mechanisms,
- actuators,
- structural design,
- mechanical advantage,
- feedback and control systems.

This ensured students understood the engineering principles behind the systems they would later construct.

**Outcome**

- Students prepared for practical development.

---

## Phase 5 – Subsystem Development

The project was divided into independent subsystems to simplify development, testing, and troubleshooting.

The following subsystems were developed:

- renewable power station,
- battery pack,
- flat conveyor,
- oscillating siever,
- slant conveyor,
- transport truck,
- Android controller,
- embedded control system.

Each subsystem was tested independently before integration.

**Outcome**

- Individual subsystem functionality verified.

---

## Phase 6 – System Integration

After individual testing, all subsystems were integrated into a complete mechatronic system.

Wireless communication was established between the Android application, the mobile truck, and the stationary processing hub.

Integration testing confirmed coordinated operation between the electrical, mechanical, and embedded subsystems.

**Outcome**

- Complete system operational.

---

## Phase 7 – Engineering Challenges

Several engineering challenges emerged during development.

Major challenges included:

- Arduino Uno pin limitations.
- Communication module selection.
- Motor driver troubleshooting.
- Android control issues.
- Bluetooth message synchronization.
- Truck steering performance.
- Linkage lifting mechanism redesign.
- Equipment loss during project development.
- Wiring faults discovered before demonstration.
- Battery failure during competition.

Each challenge required investigation, testing, redesign, and validation before the project could progress.

**Outcome**

- Multiple design improvements implemented.
- System reliability significantly improved before competition.

---

## Phase 8 – Competition Demonstration

The completed prototype successfully demonstrated:

- automated cassava sieving,
- material transportation,
- renewable energy integration,
- wireless control,
- subsystem coordination.

During the competition, the renewable power system experienced an unexpected battery failure, preventing completion of the final demonstration sequence.

Despite this setback, the project successfully demonstrated the practical integration of mechanical systems, embedded control, renewable energy, and wireless communication into a functional agricultural automation prototype.

**Outcome**

- Functional prototype demonstrated.
- Valuable engineering experience gained.

---

# Summary

The Agro-Smart Hub project progressed from a childhood observation of traditional cassava processing into a multidisciplinary mechatronic system integrating mechanical design, electrical engineering, renewable energy, embedded systems, wireless communication, and mobile application development.

The project reinforced the importance of iterative design, systematic testing, interdisciplinary collaboration, and engineering resilience when developing complex integrated systems.
