# Mechanical System

## Overview

The Mechanical System forms the physical foundation of the Agro-Smart Hub. It was designed to automate the movement and handling of cassava during the sieving stage of processing. The system consists of several interconnected mechanisms that transport, separate, and discharge cassava with minimal manual intervention.

The design emphasizes modular construction, ease of maintenance, and straightforward manufacturing using locally available materials.

---

# Objectives

The Mechanical System was designed to:

- Lift cassava into the sieving unit.
- Separate fine and coarse cassava particles.
- Transport sieved cassava to the truck.
- Move cassava to the frying area.
- Discharge cassava from the truck.
- Demonstrate practical industrial automation concepts.

---

# Mechanical Subsystems

The complete mechanical system consists of:

1. Slant Conveyor
2. Oscillating Siever
3. Flat Conveyor
4. Mobile Truck
5. Truck Lifting Mechanism
6. Truck Discharge Mechanism

# Mechanical Design Principles

The following engineering principles guided the design:

- Motion transfer
- Structural stability
- Load distribution
- Bearing-supported rotating shafts
- Belt conveyor mechanisms
- Four-wheel differential drive
- Linkage mechanisms
- Rack-and-pinion mechanisms
- Lead screw actuation
- Modular construction

# Mechanical Integration

Each subsystem was designed and tested independently before integration.

During integration, particular attention was given to:

- Alignment between conveyors.
- Height matching between adjacent units.
- Truck positioning beneath the flat conveyor.
- Stability during operation.
- Mechanical clearances.

Accurate dimensions were essential to ensure smooth material transfer between subsystems.

---

# Engineering Challenges

## Dimensional Accuracy

Because multiple independently constructed units had to operate together, careful dimensional planning was required.

Measurements were repeatedly checked before fabrication to ensure proper alignment during final assembly.

---

## Weight Distribution

After the lifting mechanism was installed on the truck, the additional weight reduced steering performance.

Rather than redesigning the chassis, the turning speed was increased, providing sufficient torque for reliable turning.

---

## Linkage Design

The first lifting mechanism worked correctly without load but failed when carrying cassava.

Analysis showed that the force transmission geometry limited the available lifting force.

The linkage geometry was redesigned by repositioning a critical joint and introducing an additional rack-and-pinion stage.

The improved mechanism successfully lifted loaded cassava.

---

# Testing

Mechanical testing included:

- Conveyor operation.
- Material transfer.
- Sieving motion.
- Truck mobility.
- Turning performance.
- Lifting mechanism.
- Discharge mechanism.
- Full system integration.

Each subsystem was validated individually before integrated testing.

---

# Lessons Learned

The Mechanical System demonstrated that successful engineering depends on careful integration rather than the performance of individual components alone.

Many of the project's most significant improvements resulted from iterative mechanical refinement rather than replacing components.

The redesign of the lifting mechanism reinforced the importance of understanding force transmission, linkage geometry, and mechanical advantage when developing new mechanisms.
